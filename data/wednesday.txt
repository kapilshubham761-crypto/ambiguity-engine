# Wednesday — Poetry (high-ambiguity, metaphor bridges)
# Phase 8 diet experiment · Day 3
# Mix of originals and paraphrased lines from public-domain / classic poetry

Grief is a country with no roads, only weather.
The clock is a mouth that eats time and calls it keeping.
She carried silence the way rivers carry winter light — without effort, without choice.
Memory is the architecture of a house that burned before you were born.
Love arrives like a power outage: total, and strangely peaceful.
Every door I open leads to a field of abandoned instruments.
The word for longing has no translation. It tastes like iron and bread.
He dreamed in colours that do not exist outside of fever.
Stars are the punctuation of a sentence no one has finished writing.
Time is a dog that eats itself in the corner of the room.
The moon dissolves in rust; the clock forgets its hands.
A grief ago, the fats and flower, the scythe-sided thorn of her voice.
What the living carry: the precise weight of an open door.
Silence is not empty — it is full of answers to questions you never learned to ask.
The sea keeps its theology. It does not explain. It arrives.
I have been many rooms. Most of them are still dark.
Death is just time becoming suddenly personal.
The body knows what the mind refuses: that hunger and beauty are the same verb.
Language is a bridge that burns the moment it holds your weight.
We are all just trying to make something permanent out of what is already leaving.
